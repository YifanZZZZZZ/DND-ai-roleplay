# Continuity and Blocking Rules

## Asset selection

Create an asset record only when at least one approved scene needs stable visual identity or state tracking. Background extras may remain a controlled group unless an individual performs a story action.

## Characters

Separate stable identity from state:

- **Identity anchors:** species or human type, age band, facial structure, hair shape, body proportions, and indispensable silhouette traits explicitly determined by the story.
- **State:** age phase, wardrobe set, carried items, dirt or injury, emotional baseline, and scene range.
- **Allowed variations:** expression, pose, temporary outer layer, natural fabric wear, or other changes that do not affect identity.
- **Forbidden variations:** identity drift, unexplained costume replacement, changing physical traits, missing story props, or state changes occurring before their cause.

When the screenplay leaves visual design unspecified, record `design_choice_pending` rather than inventing a locked attribute.

## Locations

Define world-space layout before screen direction:

- fixed landmarks and functional zones;
- entrances, exits, doors, windows, roads, work areas, and important furniture;
- adjacency and plausible travel paths;
- time, weather, crowd, damage, and occupancy states;
- elements that must remain fixed across scenes;
- elements allowed to decline, move, or disappear as part of the story.

Reuse a location with explicit states instead of creating a new location for every time period.

## Props

Track props that recur, trigger a beat, transfer between people, change condition, or establish time and continuity. Record initial owner or position, state changes, final state, and all relevant scenes.

## Blocking

Blocking describes what happens in the world, independent of cameras.

For each scene:

1. establish a simple coordinate convention tied to fixed landmarks;
2. place every active subject and story prop;
3. define a stable interaction axis;
4. split action into causal units;
5. record path, target, contact, transfer, and end position;
6. propagate the ending world state forward.

Use concrete relations such as `beside the stove`, `two steps from the bed`, `moves from doorway to cabinet`, or `faces the seated child`. Avoid unsupported precision such as exact centimeters.

Do not put turn, walk, pick up, sit, gesture, speak, and react into one action unit. Separate changes when a video model would need to resolve multiple independent body mechanics.

## Axis

Define an axis from the dominant relationship or travel direction. Preserve subject ordering and look direction across coverage. If a crossing is necessary, document one of these resets:

- a neutral view on the axis;
- a visible continuous camera move across the axis;
- a new establishing view that clearly reorients the audience;
- a scene transition that establishes new geography.
